Made for the people who sit beside others.

In hospices, hospitals, homes and counselling rooms, a single question can open a conversation that’s hard to start alone. The cards offer a gentle way in, and a simple structure to hold it.

What is Death Bed Questions?

Do you hold space for people facing the hardest conversations of their lives?

This guide shows how the Death Bed Questions decks can be used in one-to-one and group settings. It uses the same cards as every other deck, with added structure, consent and care.

The decks are a tool for reflection, conversation and meaning-making. They are not a clinical or diagnostic tool.

Principles for safe practice

Essential for everyone, whatever your role.

However you use the cards, these principles help keep every conversation safe, respectful and in the person’s control.

  1. Create safety

    Build emotional and psychological safety before the first card is drawn.

  2. Respect consent and boundaries

    Explain what the cards are, and agree together whether and how to use them.

  3. Know when to pause or stop

    Watch for signs that someone needs a break, and make stopping easy.

  4. Be mindful of grief, trauma and vulnerability

    Questions about a life can touch deep places. Go gently.

  5. Stay within your role

    Work within the boundaries of your professional role and training.

  6. Signpost when needed

    Know when to refer on or seek additional support, and where to find it.

  7. Let them stay in control

    The person being supported decides what, and how much, they share.

The facilitator’s role is to hold space, not diagnose, interrogate or advise.

A note on responsibility

Death Bed Questions is a conversation resource. It is not a substitute for professional judgement, training, safeguarding procedures or legal responsibilities. Each practitioner remains responsible for using it safely, appropriately and within the standards and requirements of their own profession and setting.

These questions can act as a doorway to healing, reconciliation and peace.
