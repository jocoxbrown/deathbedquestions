For professionals

Death Bed Questions in your practice.

A facilitator’s guide for professionals who support people through illness, loss and the end of life.

Do you hold space for people facing the hardest conversations of their lives?

This guide shows how the Death Bed Questions decks can be used in one-to-one and group settings. It uses the same cards as every other deck, with added structure, consent and care.

The decks are a tool for reflection, conversation and meaning-making. They are not a clinical or diagnostic tool.

Who it’s for

Made for the people who sit beside others.

  • Therapists
  • Counsellors
  • Death doulas
  • Hospice teams
  • Palliative care
  • Grief workers

In each of these settings, a single question can open a conversation that’s hard to start alone. The cards give clients a gentle way in, and give you a structure to hold it.

Principles for safe practice

Trauma-informed, from the first card to the last.

When the decks are used in professional settings, ethical and trauma-informed practice comes first.

  1. Consent

    Explain what the cards are and agree how you’ll use them before you begin.

  2. Pacing

    Let the client set the speed. One card may be enough for a whole session.

  3. Emotional safety

    Make it clear they can pause, pass or stop at any time.

  4. No pressure

    Never push for disclosure. What isn’t shared is still part of the work.

  5. Aftercare

    Close gently, check in on how they are, and offer containment before they leave.

  6. Referral

    If deep trauma surfaces, refer on to the right specialist support.

The facilitator’s role is to hold space, not diagnose, interrogate or advise.

Session formats

Two ways a session might look.

One-to-one session

  1. GroundBegin with a few slow breaths together.
  2. DrawThe client draws one card, or chooses from a deck.
  3. ReflectAllow a moment of silence before anyone speaks.
  4. ShareThe client shares as much or as little as they wish.
  5. CloseEnd with a short closing ritual, and suggest journalling afterwards.

Group session

  1. Set intentionsAgree the ground rules and why you’re gathered.
  2. Pass the deckThe deck moves around the circle.
  3. DrawEach person draws one card.
  4. ShareAn optional sharing circle; passing is always allowed.
  5. CloseFinish together with a closing blessing.

These questions can act as a doorway to healing, reconciliation and peace.
